Simple country bread. This loaf needs only flour, water, salt and a little yeast, but it asks for patience. Mix five hundred grams of bread flour with three hundred and fifty grams of lukewarm water until no dry flour remains, then cover the bowl and leave it for half an hour so the flour can absorb the water. Sprinkle ten grams of fine salt and two grams of instant yeast over the dough and work them in with a wet hand, squeezing the dough between your fingers and folding it over itself until the salt has dissolved and the dough feels smooth again.

Over the next two hours, give the dough a set of folds every thirty minutes. For each set, wet your hand, take the edge of the dough, stretch it up until you feel resistance and fold it over the middle, then turn the bowl a quarter turn and repeat until you have gone all the way round. After the last set, leave the dough covered until it has grown by about half and shows a few bubbles on the surface, which can take one to three hours depending on the temperature of the room.

Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and shape it into a tight ball by pulling the outside under itself. Place it seam side up in a bowl lined with a well floured cloth, cover it, and put it in the refrigerator overnight. The cold rise develops flavour and makes the dough much easier to score.

The next morning, put a heavy pot with a lid into the oven and heat it to two hundred and fifty degrees for forty five minutes. Turn the dough out onto a piece of baking paper, cut a single confident slash across the top with a sharp blade, and lower it into the hot pot using the paper. Bake with the lid on for twenty minutes, then remove the lid, lower the heat to two hundred and twenty degrees, and bake for another twenty to twenty five minutes until the crust is deep brown. Let the loaf cool for at least an hour before cutting it.
